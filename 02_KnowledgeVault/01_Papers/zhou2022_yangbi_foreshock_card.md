# Research Problem / 研究问题

Is the Yangbi foreshock-mainshock sequence explained by cascade triggering alone, or do aseismic processes (pre-slip, fluid migration) also contribute to the triggering mechanism?

# Main Contribution / 主要贡献

Showed Yangbi sequence is primarily cascade-triggering but with significant aseismic slip contributions. First foreshock preceded by localized cluster, three repeater sequences detected, aftershock zones expand logarithmically. Mainshock triggered by multiple foreshocks through both seismic and aseismic processes.

# Method Overview / 方法概述

1. **Fault structure**: Determined via relocated aftershocks and multipoint-source inversion
2. **Rupture area**: Estimated from aftershock location + spectral ratio analysis for each foreshock
3. **Coulomb stress**: Calculated evolution to examine interactions between events
4. **Aseismic detection**: Identified pre-slip cluster, repeater sequences, logarithmic aftershock expansion

# Dataset and Evaluation / 数据集与评估

- **Foreshocks**: 05/18 M 4.3, 05/19 M 4.6, 05/21 M 5.2 (before mainshock 05/21 M 6.1)
- **Fault geometry**: Mainshock + two smaller foreshocks on unmapped near-vertical fault; largest foreshock on mapped stepover fault (NE-dipping)
- **Evidence**: Cascade triggering + aseismic slip indicators

# Why This Paper Matters / 为什么关注这篇论文

Foreshock mechanism is a fundamental question in seismology. The Yangbi sequence provides a rare well-recorded example of a cascade sequence with aseismic contributions. Relevant for understanding earthquake preparation processes and early warning.

# Limitations / 局限性

- Focuses on one sequence — limited statistical generality
- Aseismic slip inference relies on indirect indicators
- Spectral ratio analysis has inherent uncertainties
- Multipoint-source inversion requires assumptions about source geometry
